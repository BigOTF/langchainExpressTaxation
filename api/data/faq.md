# Nigeria Tax FAQ (plain-language summary, effective 1 January 2026)

Note: This is a summary of the Nigeria Tax Act 2025 and the Nigeria Tax Administration Act 2025. If it conflicts with the Acts, the Acts win.

---

## Which laws now govern tax in Nigeria?
Four laws signed on 26 June 2025 took effect on 1 January 2026: the Nigeria Tax Act (what is taxed, rates, exemptions), the Nigeria Tax Administration Act (registration, filing, penalties, offences), the Nigeria Revenue Service (Establishment) Act, and the Joint Revenue Board (Establishment) Act. The Nigeria Tax Act replaced the Personal Income Tax Act, Companies Income Tax Act, Capital Gains Tax Act, VAT Act and Stamp Duties Act.

---

## Which body collects tax now? Is FIRS still there?
The Federal Inland Revenue Service (FIRS) was replaced by the Nigeria Revenue Service (NRS), which collects federal taxes such as company income tax, VAT and petroleum tax. State Internal Revenue Services (for example LIRS in Lagos) collect personal income tax from individuals.

---

## What is the minimum income that is taxed? What is the tax-free threshold? Who does not pay income tax?
The first ₦800,000 of annual taxable (chargeable) income is taxed at 0%. A person whose chargeable income is ₦800,000 or less pays no personal income tax. This replaces the old system where tax started from the first ₦300,000.

---

## What are the personal income tax rates and bands? What is the highest tax rate?
Rates are progressive, so each rate applies only to the slice of income in that band:
- First ₦800,000: 0%
- Next ₦2,200,000 (up to ₦3 million): 15%
- Next ₦9,000,000 (up to ₦12 million): 18%
- Next ₦13,000,000 (up to ₦25 million): 21%
- Next ₦25,000,000 (up to ₦50 million): 23%
- Above ₦50 million: 25%
The highest rate is 25%.

---

## How do I calculate my personal income tax? Show an example.
Step 1: Take gross income. Step 2: Subtract allowed deductions (pension, NHF, rent relief). The result is chargeable income. Step 3: Apply the bands to each slice.
Example: gross income ₦5,000,000, rent paid ₦1,200,000. Rent relief = 20% of ₦1,200,000 = ₦240,000. Chargeable income = ₦4,760,000. Tax: first ₦800,000 at 0% = ₦0; next ₦2,200,000 at 15% = ₦330,000; remaining ₦1,760,000 at 18% = ₦316,800. Total tax = ₦646,800.
Example: chargeable income ₦1,000,000. First ₦800,000 at 0% = ₦0; remaining ₦200,000 at 15% = ₦30,000. Total tax = ₦30,000.

---

## What is rent relief? What happened to the Consolidated Relief Allowance (CRA)?
The Consolidated Relief Allowance (20% of gross income plus ₦200,000 or 1% of gross income) was abolished. It was replaced by rent relief under section 30: 20% of the annual rent you pay, capped at ₦500,000. You need proof such as a tenancy agreement or receipts. People who do not pay rent cannot claim it.

---

## Is gift money, a loan, or a remittance from abroad taxable income?
Under the Nigeria Tax Act 2025, remittances, gifts, loans and bank deposits are not treated as taxable income by themselves.

---

## Who must pay personal income tax? Are residents taxed on foreign income?
Tax residents of Nigeria are taxed on their worldwide income. Non-residents are taxed on income from Nigerian sources.

---

## What is capital gains tax (CGT)? What is the CGT rate?
Gains on disposal of assets are now taxed at the normal income tax rates. For individuals that means the personal income tax bands. For companies the rate is 30%, up from the old flat 10%. The Act also covers indirect transfers of shares in Nigerian companies. Gains on the sale of shares are exempt if proceeds in any 12 consecutive months are ₦150 million or less and the gain does not exceed ₦10 million.

---

## What is a small company? Do small businesses pay tax?
A small company has annual turnover of ₦100 million or less and fixed assets below ₦250 million (professional service businesses are excluded). Under section 56, small companies are exempt from company income tax, capital gains tax and the development levy. They must still register and file returns.

---

## What is the company income tax rate? How much do companies pay?
Small companies pay 0%. Other companies pay 30% on assessable profit (some commentators report a move to 25%, so confirm against the Act). Companies other than small companies also pay the development levy.

---

## What is the development levy?
Under section 59, a 4% development levy on assessable profits applies to companies other than small companies and non-resident companies. It replaced the Tertiary Education Tax, NASENI levy, IT levy and Police Trust Fund levy.

---

## What is the VAT rate? Which items are VAT exempt?
VAT is 7.5%. Items exempt or zero-rated include basic food items, medical services, pharmaceuticals, educational services (school fees), baby products, and exports.

---

## Do I need a Tax ID? Who must register for tax?
Every taxable person (individuals earning income, businesses and companies) must register with the relevant tax authority and obtain a Tax Identification Number (Tax ID). A Tax ID cannot be transferred to another person and is required for government contracts. Companies that award contracts to unregistered vendors can be penalised.

---

## When must I file my tax return? What is the filing deadline?
Individuals file annual returns by 31 March of the following year (state revenue services such as LIRS enforce this under section 13 of the Nigeria Tax Administration Act). Companies file within the deadlines in the Administration Act.

---

## What is the penalty for not filing a tax return or filing late?
Under section 101 of the Nigeria Tax Administration Act, failure to file a return attracts a penalty of ₦100,000 in the first month and ₦50,000 for each further month the failure continues. Filing an incorrect return is treated the same way.

---

## What is the penalty for not registering for tax?
A taxable person who fails to register is liable to a fine of ₦50,000 in the first month and ₦25,000 for each further month of default.

---

## What is the punishment for tax evasion? What are the offences and penalties?
Tax evasion (deliberately hiding income, false returns, fake expense claims, refusing to issue e-invoices) is an offence under the Nigeria Tax Administration Act. Chapter Four of the Act sets out offences and penalties, including false statements and returns, incorrect accounts, failure to comply with notices, counterfeiting documents, and offences by a body corporate. Penalties include administrative fines, interest on unpaid tax, imprisonment and, for businesses, loss of licence. Examples of penalties in the Act:
- Offences by authorised and unauthorised persons: a fine of 200% of the sum involved, or up to three years in prison, or both.
- Fraud relating to stamps: up to three years in prison or a fine of at least ₦2,000,000, or both.
- Aiding and abetting an offence: ₦1,000,000 or up to three years in prison, or both.
- Obstructing a tax officer: ₦1,000,000 penalty and, on conviction, a fine up to ₦1,000,000 or imprisonment.
- Inducing (bribing) an authorised officer: a fine up to ₦1,000,000 or up to three years in prison, or both.
- Physically assaulting a tax officer: a fine of ₦10 million and up to 10 years in prison.
Confirm exact amounts against the section cited in the Act.

---

## Is tax avoidance different from tax evasion?
Yes. Tax avoidance is legal planning, such as claiming reliefs the law allows. Tax evasion is illegal: hiding income, false records, or fake claims.

---

## What happens if I do not pay my tax on time? Is there interest?
Late payment attracts interest and penalties under the Nigeria Tax Administration Act. Interest is linked to the Central Bank of Nigeria monetary policy rate. Tax authorities can also collect through enforcement powers such as distraint.

---

## Are crypto and digital assets taxed?
Yes. Gains from digital assets are taxable, and virtual asset service providers (VASPs) have registration, KYC and reporting duties under the Administration Act. A VASP that fails to comply can face an administrative fine of ₦10 million.
